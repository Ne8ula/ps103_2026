## Step 1. Wet your hands and apply soap

`0:00–0:13` · `s01` · confidence 0.90

Wet your hands under running water and then apply soap using a dispenser.

- Ensure hands are fully wet before applying soap.
- Use the soap dispenser to place a portion of soap onto your palm.

**Tools:** sink, faucet, soap dispenser  
**Materials:** running water, soap  

**Expected result:** Hands are wet and coated with soap.

**Compliance:**
- **[requirement, warning]** Use warm water when wetting your hands to ensure the soap works effectively.  
  _Source: FDA Food Code 2022 p.346 — “a warm water temperature is important in achieving the maximum surfactant effect of the soap.”_ (`d01:622`)
- **[requirement, warning]** Before beginning the handwashing procedure, retrieve a paper towel to use later for turning off the faucet and opening doors to avoid recontamination.  
  _Source: FDA Food Code 2022 p.346 — “This can be accomplished by obtaining a paper towel from its dispenser before the handwashing procedure”_ (`d01:623`)
- **[requirement, warning]** Ensure you are using a designated handwashing sink; washing in sinks used for food preparation or warewashing is not permitted.  
  _Source: FDA Food Code 2022 p.62 — “FOOD EMPLOYEES shall clean their hands in a HANDWASHING SINK or APPROVED automatic handwashing facility and may not clean their hands in a sink used for FOOD preparation or WAREWASHING”_ (`d01:80`)

## Step 2. Scrub hands and wrists for 20 seconds

`0:13–0:28` · `s02` · confidence 1.00

Rub your hands together for at least 20 seconds to ensure a thorough clean. Scrub the backs of your hands, between your fingers, your fingertips, under your nails, your thumbs, and your wrists.

- Interlace your fingers to clean the spaces between them.
- Pay close attention to scrubbing under the fingernails and around the thumbs.
- Extend the scrubbing action up to your wrists.

**Tools:** Sink  
**Materials:** Soap  

**Expected result:** Both hands and wrists are completely covered in soap lather.

**Compliance:**
- **[requirement]** The most important part of the process is the abrasive action created by vigorously rubbing your hands together to loosen transient microorganisms.  
  _Source: FDA Food Code 2022 p.346 — “It is the abrasive action obtained by vigorously rubbing the surfaces being cleaned that loosens the transient microorganisms on the hands.”_ (`d01:622`)
- **[requirement, warning]** While scrubbing, you must pay particular attention to removing any soil from underneath your fingernails.  
  _Source: FDA Food Code 2022 p.61 — “Paying particular attention to removing soil from underneath the fingernails during the cleaning procedure”_ (`d01:78`)
- **[requirement]** The vigorous rubbing phase must last at least 10 to 15 seconds, but the total cleaning procedure from start to finish must take at least 20 seconds.  
  _Source: FDA Food Code 2022 p.61 — “FOOD EMPLOYEES shall clean their hands and exposed portions of their arms, including surrogate prosthetic devices for hands or arms for at least 20 seconds”; FDA Food Code 2022 p.61 — “Rub together vigorously for at least 10 to 15 seconds”_ (`d01:78, d01:78`)
- **[caution, warning]** Never wash your hands in a food preparation sink, as this practice can lead to avoidable contamination of the sink and food products.  
  _Source: FDA Food Code 2022 p.347 — “It is inappropriate to wash hands in a food preparation sink since this may result in avoidable contamination of the sink and the food prepared therein.”_ (`d01:626`)

## Step 3. Rinse and dry your hands ⚠ needs info from uploader

`0:28–0:44` · `s03` · confidence 0.90

Rinse your hands thoroughly and dry them completely using a paper towel, air dryer, or cloth towel.

- Use a paper towel to grasp the door handle when exiting a public restroom to maintain cleanliness.
- A clean cloth towel is suitable for drying hands when at home.

**Tools:** paper towels, air dryer, door handle, towel  
**Materials:** water  

**Expected result:** Hands are clean and dry.

**Compliance:**
- **[conflict, critical]** The instructions suggest using a cloth towel at home, but regulations require specific hand-drying methods such as individual disposable towels or a continuous towel system.  
  _Source: FDA Food Code 2022 p.153 — “Each HANDWASHING SINK or group of adjacent HANDWASHING SINKS shall be provided with: (A) Individual, disposable towels; (B) A continuous towel system that supplies the user with a clean towel; or (C) A heated-air hand drying device”_ (`d01:293`)
- **[requirement, warning]** To prevent recontamination after rinsing, you should also use a paper towel to operate the sink faucet handles.  
  _Source: FDA Food Code 2022 p.346 — “This can be accomplished by obtaining a paper towel from its dispenser before the handwashing procedure, then, after handwashing, using the paper towel to operate the hand sink faucet handles and restroom door handles.”_ (`d01:623`)
- **[reference]** Ensuring hands are completely dry is vital as moisture increases the risk of transferring bacteria and viruses to other surfaces.  
  _Source: FDA Food Code 2022 p.482 — “The residual moisture found on the hands after washing allows for bacterial and viral transfer to food or solid surfaces by touch.”_ (`d01:926`)
- **[requirement, warning]** When rinsing your hands, you must use clean, running warm water.  
  _Source: FDA Food Code 2022 p.61 — “(4) Thoroughly rinse under clean, running warm water;”_ (`d01:78`)

## Step 4. Use hand sanitizer if soap is unavailable ⚠ needs info from uploader

`0:44–0:58` · `s04` · confidence 0.90

Apply an alcohol-based hand sanitizer to your hands and rub them together until dry.

- Ensure the sanitizer contains at least 60% alcohol.
- Cover all surfaces of your hands during rubbing.

**Tools:** Hand sanitizer pump bottle  
**Materials:** Alcohol-based hand sanitizer  

**Expected result:** Hands are coated in sanitizer and rubbed until completely dry.

**Compliance:**
- **[conflict, critical]** Hand sanitizer cannot be used as a substitute for washing with soap; regulations require that each handwashing sink be provided with a supply of hand cleaning soap for employee use.  
  _Source: FDA Food Code 2022 p.153 — “Each HANDWASHING SINK or group of 2 adjacent HANDWASHING SINKS shall be provided with a supply of hand cleaning liquid, powder, or bar soap.”_ (`d01:293`)
- **[requirement, warning]** The hand antiseptic used must be an FDA-approved drug or contain active antimicrobial ingredients listed in the FDA monograph to ensure safety and effectiveness.  
  _Source: FDA Food Code 2022 p.62 — “A hand antiseptic used as a topical application, a hand antiseptic solution used as a hand dip, or a hand antiseptic soap shall: (1) Comply with one of the following: (a) Be an APPROVED drug that is listed in the FDA publication Approved Drug Products with Therapeutic Equivalence Evaluations”_ (`d01:80`)
- **[reference]** Regulatory standards use the term "hand antiseptic" instead of "hand sanitizer" because skin agents typically achieve a much lower microbial reduction than the 5-log standard required for equipment.  
  _Source: FDA Food Code 2022 p.347 — “The Food Code definition of "sanitizer" requires a minimum microbial reduction of 5 logs, which is equal to a 99.999% reduction.”_ (`d01:627`)
- **[caution, warning]** Ensure your hands are completely dry before proceeding, as residual moisture on the skin allows for easier transfer of bacteria and viruses to food and surfaces.  
  _Source: FDA Food Code 2022 p.482 — “It is known that wet hands transfer bacteria more readily than dry hands.”_ (`d01:926`)
